<h2 style="text-align: justify;">What Is the Salesforce AP-203 Exam?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">The Salesforce AP-203 exam is associated with the Salesforce Accredited Agentforce Communications Professional credential. It is designed for professionals involved in evaluating customer requirements and planning or delivering solutions for communications-industry businesses.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">The exam covers three major areas: Discovery, Planning and Design, and Delivery. Planning and Design carries the largest weighting, so candidates should give particular attention to solution architecture, product catalog design, order processes, asset management, and migration planning.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">Which AP-203 Exam Topics Deserve the Most Attention?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">The exam outline is divided into three domains. Their weightings can help you decide how to distribute your study time. Planning and Design represents 60% of the exam, making it the largest area to focus on.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: justify;">1. Discovery &ndash; 24%</h3>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Discovery is about understanding the customer&#39;s business before deciding how to build a solution. You need to recognize business problems, technical limitations, and the outcomes a customer wants to achieve.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Review these areas:</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Assessing an existing IT environment and solution architecture</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Identifying business goals, pain points, budgets, and KPIs</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Selecting suitable Agentforce Communications products</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Mapping customer personas, business processes, and requirements</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Understanding data volumes and platform constraints</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Identifying when native functionality or an external solution is appropriate</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">For better preparation, practise reading a customer scenario and identifying the information you would need before recommending a solution.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: justify;">2. Planning and Design &ndash; 60%</h3>

<p style="text-align: justify;">This domain carries the highest exam weighting. It tests your ability to turn business requirements into a scalable solution design.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Important areas include:</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Designing and organizing scalable product catalogs</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Understanding commercial product decomposition</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Planning order capture and quoting processes</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Designing order fulfillment and orchestration flows</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Creating data migration strategies</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Prioritizing user stories and identifying sprint dependencies</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Designing asset management and MACD flows</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">MACD refers to moves, adds, changes, and disconnects. Understand how these activities affect asset management and the customer lifecycle.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Don&#39;t study these concepts as unrelated definitions. Consider how a change in a customer&#39;s product or service could affect the catalog, order, fulfillment process, and existing assets.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: justify;">3. Delivery &ndash; 16%</h3>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Delivery focuses on turning an approved solution design into an implementation plan. You should understand which requirements can be handled through standard functionality and where customization may be necessary.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Concentrate on:</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Comparing out-of-the-box functionality with custom development</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Implementing architecture and product catalog components</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Delivering order capture and order management functionality</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Planning migration from legacy systems</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Maintaining data quality during implementation</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Scenario-based practice is particularly useful here. Given a set of requirements, try to explain how you would move from the proposed design to a workable implementation.</p>
